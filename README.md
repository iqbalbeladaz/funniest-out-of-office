## The entry

> I'm out of the office until Monday. My inbox is being guarded by an intern named Denial — he opens everything and answers nothing.

One line, 130 characters (gate: 1–140).

## Why this line should win

- **A reply a real person would set:** it opens like every out-of-office
  note — away until Monday — then turns on the one detail nobody
  expects.
- **A genuine surprise:** the inbox has a guard, and the guard is an
  intern named Denial — a stage of grief, hired as staff. The twist is
  invisible until the name lands.
- **A voice of its own:** dry, self-aware, a little weary of its own
  inbox. No template phrasing anywhere in it.
- **Every word earns its place:** "he opens everything and answers
  nothing" is exactly what an inbox does while its owner is away — the
  punchline is also a true description.
- **Fresh:** no "your email is important to me", no "limited access",
  no beach joke. Nobody else's auto-reply has an intern called Denial.

## The program

`main.py` writes the line to `/output/entry.txt`. Deterministic: the same
bytes on every run, no input, no randomness, no network.
